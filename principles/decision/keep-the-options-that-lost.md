# Keep the options that lost

**Claim:** a decision record lists every option considered, the chosen one included, with its consequences and why the others lost.

- **Grade:** convention, adopted; one writer-side experiment.
- **Evidence:** [design-decisions review](../../evidence/design-decisions/review.md); [state of the art](../../evidence/design-decisions/state-of-the-art.md).
- **Check:** `shape-conformance` (exact): a record holds an Options section; `decision-records` (exact): it names its shape.
- **Reader styles:** none particular to this genre yet; the reader-ergonomics styles hold.

## What it asks

- After the decision, a table: each option, what it would bring, and whether it was chosen or why it lost.
- Keep the debate itself in the design document, and link it.

## Why

- In one laboratory experiment, recorded rationale made later decisions more effective when the requirements changed
  (Falessi 2010, 50 students).
- MADR 2024 adds the options to Nygard's record; participants found its rationale deeper and slower to write
  (Nogueira 2026, a preprint, 33 undergraduates).

## Limits

- The one comparison of templates measured the writer, not the reader.
- Whether the options reduce repeated debate is claimed by PEP 1 and untested.
- The cost of capture is the barrier named longest for recording rationale (Lee 1997; Tang 2006): keep it short.
