# The decision asked comes first

**Claim:** a design document opens with the decision it asks of the reviewer, before the motivation.

- **Grade:** convention, untested (a hypothesis to test).
- **Evidence:** [design-decisions baseline, the candidate variant](../../evidence/design-decisions/baseline.md); [position](../../evidence/design-decisions/position.md).
- **Check:** `design-docs` (exact): the first labelled part after the title is the Decision asked.
- **Reader styles:** none particular to this genre yet; the reader-ergonomics styles hold.

## What it asks

- Under the title, one sentence: what the reviewer must decide, and by when.
- Then the status, then a summary of a few lines, then the motivation.

## Why

- A reviewer reads with a question; stating it first is the [overview first](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/7fa4af5d5cf4ddf3cba7076d9d95e3cd96362762/principles/document/overview-first.md)
  principle applied to a proposal.
- The proposal templates compared share motivation, design and alternatives; none puts the decision asked first, which
  is why this is a hypothesis and not a finding.

## Limits

- No experiment was found in which reviewers judged a proposal in two forms.
- Go proposals were declined most often for feasibility, use cases and principles, not for their form (Kondo 2025): the
  form may matter less than the content.
